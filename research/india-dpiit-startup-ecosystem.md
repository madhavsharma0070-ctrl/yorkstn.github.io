## 1. Scope, methodology, and disclaimer

This document summarizes **publicly published** eligibility criteria, benefits, and mechanics of the following official Indian government startup-support frameworks, as they stood at the time of this research (July 2026):

1. DPIIT (Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade) Startup Recognition
2. Startup India Seed Fund Scheme (SISFS) and the Fund of Funds for Startups (FFS)
3. Startup Haryana (Haryana State Startup Policy)
4. Other national programmes that could plausibly be relevant to a B2B SaaS / enterprise-AI company (MeitY Startup Hub / SAMRIDH, Digital India, PLI schemes)

**Methodology.** Research was conducted via web search and attempted direct retrieval of primary government sources (`startupindia.gov.in`, `dpiit.gov.in`, `ipindia.gov.in`, `pib.gov.in`, `startupharyana.gov.in`, `incometaxindia.gov.in`). **Direct automated fetching of these government domains was blocked by their web-application firewalls/bot-protection during this research session (HTTP 403 on every attempt, including PDFs and the Wayback Machine mirror).** As a result, the citations below reference the official URLs as the authoritative source of record, but the specific facts stated were extracted through search-engine indexing/snippets and cross-checked against **multiple independent secondary sources** (law firm client alerts, tax/compliance advisory sites, and news coverage of official announcements) that themselves cite and quote the primary sources. Every material claim below is flagged with its source(s). Where sources disagreed, were ambiguous, or could not be corroborated by more than one independent write-up, this is explicitly marked **"Requires further validation."**

**This is not legal or tax advice, and it is not a claim of eligibility for any scheme.** Indian startup policy changed materially in 2024–2026 (angel tax abolished; DPIIT thresholds raised; a new "Deep Tech Startup" category created; SIPP scheme lapsed). Thresholds, portals, and scheme statuses change; **before relying on any figure in this document for a decision, the reader should verify current status directly on the official portal** (`startupindia.gov.in`, `dpiit.gov.in`, `nsws.gov.in`, `startupharyana.gov.in`) or with a qualified Indian company-secretary/tax advisor.

## 2. DPIIT Startup Recognition — official definition & eligibility criteria

### 2.1 Legal basis

DPIIT recognition is governed by periodic Gazette notifications under the Startup India initiative. The framework in effect for most of 2019–2026 was **Gazette Notification G.S.R. 127(E) dated 19 February 2019**. According to multiple legal-update sources, this was **superseded by Gazette Notification G.S.R. 108(E) dated 4 February 2026**, which introduced a new "Deep Tech Startup" sub-category and raised turnover/age thresholds. [Lexology summary](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=44ee6234-e278-4514-a791-7f72f061a7f0); [Mondaq summary](https://www.mondaq.com/india/corporate-and-company-law/1745448/indias-new-startup-and-deep-tech-startup-framework-why-the-2026-dpiit-notification-matters); [TaxGuru summary](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/comprehensive-guide-indias-2026-startup-redefinition.html); official notice text should be independently confirmed at [dpiit.gov.in](https://dpiit.gov.in/) — **Requires further validation** that this is the exact current in-force text, since the primary Gazette PDF could not be directly retrieved in this session.

### 2.2 Eligible entity types

Per the (pre-2026 and, per secondary sources, expanded 2026) definition, an entity is eligible for DPIIT recognition if it is incorporated/registered in India as one of:
- A **Private Limited Company** (under the Companies Act, 2013), **or**
- A **Registered Partnership Firm** (under the Partnership Act, 1932), **or**
- A **Limited Liability Partnership** (under the LLP Act, 2008)

Multiple 2026 secondary sources state that the February 2026 notification **expanded the definition to also include Cooperative Societies and multi-state cooperative societies** as eligible entity types for the first time. [Official recognition page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/comprehensive-guide-indias-2026-startup-redefinition.html); [SME Venture](https://smeventure.com/dpiit-raises-startup-turnover-to-%E2%82%B9200-cr-deep-tech-to-20-years/). **Sole proprietorships are not eligible.**

### 2.3 Age of entity

- **Regular startups:** eligible for up to **10 years from the date of incorporation/registration**.
- **Deep Tech Startups (new 2026 category):** eligible for up to **20 years from the date of incorporation/registration**. A Deep Tech Startup is described in secondary sources as one engaged in "development or commercialization of core scientific or engineering innovations involving substantial R&D," with an emphasis on IP creation (patents) rather than service-model or incrementally-improved businesses. [Official page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html); [IPR Guru](https://www.iprguru.in/ipr-basics/deep-tech-startup-definition-2026/); [MHCO legal update](https://www.mhcolaw.com/updates/legal-update-dpiit-notification-on-deep-tech-startups-2026/208). **Requires further validation** of the precise Deep Tech qualifying criteria against the primary Gazette text.

### 2.4 Turnover ceiling

- **Regular startups:** annual turnover must be **less than ₹200 crore** in any of the financial years since incorporation (this figure was raised from the earlier ₹100 crore cap by the February 2026 notification, per secondary sources).
- **Deep Tech Startups:** turnover ceiling raised to **₹300 crore**.
[Official page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html); [SME Venture](https://smeventure.com/dpiit-raises-startup-turnover-to-%E2%82%B9200-cr-deep-tech-to-20-years/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/startup-definition-india-dpiit-expands-eligibility-limits.html).

Note: the **older ₹100 crore threshold** (and the pre-2026 framework generally) is still what is quoted by many advisory pages not yet updated for the 2026 notification, and it remains the correct historical figure for anything dated 2019–early 2026 (e.g., the Section 80-IAC turnover test discussed in §3.1, which some sources still tie to ₹100 crore — **requires further validation whether 80-IAC's own turnover threshold was also raised to match the new ₹200 crore recognition threshold, or remains a separate ₹100 crore test under the Income Tax Act**).

### 2.5 Innovation / scalability requirement

The entity must be "working towards innovation, development, or improvement of products or processes or services, or if it is a scalable business model with a high potential of employment generation or wealth creation." This is the core substantive (non-financial) test DPIIT examiners apply, and it is inherently qualitative — there is no bright-line rule published for what satisfies it. [Official recognition page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html).

### 2.6 Negative condition

The entity **must not have been formed by splitting up or reconstructing an already-existing business**. This is intended to prevent existing businesses from re-registering as "new" startups to claim benefits. [Official recognition page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html).

### 2.7 Application process

- Applications are made online through the **National Single Window System (NSWS)** at `nsws.gov.in`. Multiple 2026-dated secondary sources state the older Startup India portal itself **no longer accepts new recognition applications directly** — new applications route through NSWS, where the applicant creates an account, builds an "Entity Profile," and applies for the "Registration as a Startup" / "Startup Recognition" service.
- **No government fee** is charged for the DPIIT Certificate of Recognition or Certificate of Eligibility.
- Processing time is reported (by secondary sources, not confirmed on the primary portal in this session) as roughly **7–14 days**.
[Official recognition page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html); NSWS portal: [nsws.gov.in](https://www.nsws.gov.in/) (not independently fetched in this session — **requires further validation**); official user-guide PDF referenced by search results at `startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/invest-india/NSWS Startup Application Guide.pdf` (could not be retrieved directly — 403).

## 3. DPIIT Startup Recognition — benefits

All of the following benefits require **DPIIT recognition as a prerequisite**; recognition itself does *not* automatically confer every downstream benefit — several (notably the Section 80-IAC tax holiday) require a **separate application and approval** (to the Inter-Ministerial Board, "IMB").

### 3.1 Income tax holiday — Section 80-IAC of the Income Tax Act, 1961

- Eligible startups can claim a **100% deduction of profits** for **any 3 consecutive financial years out of the first 10 years** from incorporation.
- Eligibility for 80-IAC specifically (as distinct from bare DPIIT recognition) is commonly stated as: incorporated as a Private Limited Company or LLP on or after 1 April 2016; less than 10 years old; annual turnover under ₹100 crore in any financial year; and satisfying the same innovation/scalability test as DPIIT recognition.
- **DPIIT recognition alone does not grant the 80-IAC exemption** — a separate application to the Inter-Ministerial Board (IMB) must be filed and approved.
[Official 80-IAC application page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/form80iac.html) (not independently fetched — 403; cited as the primary reference point per search results); [Official recognition/tax exemption page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html); corroborating secondary detail: [ComplianceCalendar](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/section-80iac-of-income-tax-act); PIB press release on 80-IAC approvals, referenced by search results as PRID 2128860 at `pib.gov.in` (URL could not be directly fetched in this session — **requires further validation** of the exact approval numbers cited there).

**Note on discrepancy:** several sources continue to cite an ₹100 crore turnover cap specifically for 80-IAC purposes even after the DPIIT recognition threshold moved to ₹200 crore in Feb 2026. Since 80-IAC eligibility sits in the Income Tax Act (a separate statute from the DPIIT Gazette notification), **it is not safe to assume the 80-IAC turnover cap automatically tracks the DPIIT recognition cap** — this needs to be confirmed against the Income Tax Act / CBDT circulars directly. **Requires further validation.**

### 3.2 Angel tax exemption (Section 56(2)(viib)) — now largely moot for all companies

- Historically, DPIIT-recognized startups meeting certain conditions (aggregate paid-up share capital + share premium after the proposed share issue not exceeding ₹25 crore, among others) received immunity from Section 56(2)(viib) of the Income Tax Act — the provision that otherwise taxed share premium received above "fair market value" as income (the so-called "angel tax").
- **Union Budget 2024 (Finance Act, 2024)** made Section 56(2)(viib) **inapplicable to all companies, regardless of DPIIT status, effective Assessment Year 2025-26 (i.e., from 1 April 2025)** — i.e., angel tax was **abolished economy-wide**, not just for recognized startups.
- **Practical implication:** DPIIT-specific angel tax exemption is now largely superseded by the blanket abolition; the DPIIT angel tax exemption route remains relevant chiefly for **historical assessment years still under dispute/scrutiny** (pre-FY2025-26).
[Income Tax Dept. explainer page (title indexed via search; not independently fetched — 403)](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/exemption-from-angel-tax-section-56-2-viib-); corroborating: [Business Standard on Budget 2024 abolition](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/budget/news/budget-2024-abolishes-angel-tax-on-startups-for-all-classes-of-investors-124072300667_1.html); [Legal500 summary](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/abolition-of-angel-tax-in-india-a-boost-for-the-startup-ecosystem/).

### 3.3 Self-certification for labour and environmental law compliance

- DPIIT-recognized startups may **self-certify compliance with 6 labour laws and 3 environmental laws** through an online declaration, in lieu of routine inspections.
- **Labour laws:** no inspection for **5 years** from the date of self-certification, except in response to a **credible, verifiable, written complaint** approved at least one level above the inspecting officer.
- **Environmental laws:** applies to startups falling under the Central Pollution Control Board's (CPCB) **"white category"** (i.e., minimal-pollution-potential businesses) — these may self-certify, subject only to **random checks**. (This category is generally not directly relevant to a software/SaaS company, which is unlikely to be regulated under CPCB "categories" at all, but is included here for completeness per the official benefits list.)
[Official recognition/benefits page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html); corroborating summary: [TaxGuru — Government facilities for Startups](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/government-facilities-startups.html).

### 3.4 Easier public procurement

- DPIIT-recognized startups are **exempt from Earnest Money Deposit (EMD)/bid security** requirements in central government tenders.
- Startups may participate in public-procurement tenders **without the prior-experience or minimum-turnover conditions** ordinarily required of bidders (this exemption is codified via **General Financial Rules (GFR) Rule 173(i)** per secondary sources on Government e-Marketplace (GeM) procurement).
- DPIIT-recognized startups can register on **GeM (Government e-Marketplace)** as a seller and access a startup-specific "runway"/onboarding pathway, and may bid for and win trial orders from government departments/PSUs.
[Official recognition/benefits page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html); corroborating: [GeM startup benefits summary](https://www.bidz365.com/blog/how-startup-india-registered-companies-can-benefit-on-gem); GeM portal itself: [gem.gov.in](https://gem.gov.in/) (not independently fetched — **requires further validation** of current GFR rule citation and exact mechanics).

### 3.5 IPR fast-tracking and fee rebates — Scheme for Facilitating Startups' Intellectual Property Protection (SIPP)

- Historically (since 2016), DPIIT-recognized startups received: **80% rebate on patent filing fees**, **50% rebate on trademark filing fees**, **fast-track examination of patent applications**, and free facilitator assistance (government-funded IP facilitators helping with filing/prosecution).
- **Important, time-sensitive finding:** multiple 2026-dated secondary sources (S.S. Rana & Co., Mondaq, Chambers and Partners) report that **the most recently notified version of the SIPP scheme lapsed/expired on 31 March 2026**, and that **as of mid-2026 there had been no official government announcement of an extension, renewal, or replacement**. If this is still the case at the time of reading, **new IP filings by startups may no longer receive the fee-rebate/facilitator subsidy**, and applicants would bear full professional/filing costs. **This should be verified directly on `ipindia.gov.in` before assuming SIPP benefits are currently available.**
[Original scheme document (title indexed via search; PDF not independently fetched — 403)](https://ipindia.gov.in/frontend/pdf/patents/323_1_Scheme_for_facilitating_start-ups.pdf); [S.S. Rana & Co. — "The SIPP Scheme: What Its Expiry Means for Startups"](https://ssrana.in/articles/the-sipp-scheme-what-its-expiry-means-for-startups/); [Startup India IPR benefits page (official; not independently fetched — 403)](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/intellectual-property-rights.html).

### 3.6 Access to the Fund of Funds for Startups (indirect)

See §4.2 below — DPIIT recognition is a prerequisite for a startup to be an eligible investee of AIFs backed by the government's Fund of Funds, but recognition alone confers no direct funding.

## 4. Startup India Seed Fund Scheme (SISFS) and Fund of Funds for Startups (FFS)

### 4.1 Startup India Seed Fund Scheme (SISFS)

- **Launch:** April 2021, by DPIIT under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry.
- **Purpose:** early-stage grant/debt funding for **proof of concept validation, prototype development, product trials**, and market entry — explicitly aimed at the pre-Series-A / pre-institutional-VC stage.
- **Grant size:** up to **₹20 lakh** as a grant (disbursed in milestone-based tranches), with additional debt/convertible-debenture support reportedly available for market-entry stages beyond the grant (figures for the debt component were not independently confirmed in this session — **requires further validation**).
- **Eligibility criteria** (as reported by multiple secondary sources summarizing the scheme guidelines):
  - Must hold **DPIIT recognition**.
  - Must be **incorporated not more than 2 years** before the date of application.
  - Must have a business idea for a product/service with **market fit, a viable path to commercialization, and potential to scale**.
  - **At least 51% Indian promoter shareholding** at the time of application.
  - Must **not have already received more than ₹10 lakh** of monetary support under any other Central or State Government scheme (this is designed to prevent double-dipping across schemes; exact wording/thresholds should be confirmed on the primary scheme guidelines).
- **Delivery mechanism:** Startups **do not apply directly to DPIIT**. Funds are disbursed through a network of **DPIIT-selected incubators** ("Eligible Incubators"), which run their own selection/evaluation processes and disburse against milestones.
[India Science & Technology portal listing (official aggregator; not independently fetched — 403)](https://www.indiascienceandtechnology.gov.in/funding-opportunities/startups/startup-india-seed-fund-scheme-sisfs); corroborating secondary summaries: [BankBazaar](https://www.bankbazaar.com/government-schemes/startup-india-seed-fund-scheme.html); [Cashfree](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/startup-india-seed-fund-scheme-sisfs-eligibility-how-to-apply/). Official scheme home is also referenced at `seedfund.startupindia.gov.in` — **requires further validation** (not independently fetched).

### 4.2 Fund of Funds for Startups (FFS) / "Startup India FoF 2.0"

- **Structure:** FFS is **not a direct-to-startup fund**. It is a "fund of funds" — DPIIT is the monitoring/policy agency, and **SIDBI (Small Industries Development Bank of India)** is the operating agency that commits capital **into SEBI-registered Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs)**, which in turn invest into individual startups.
- **Corpus:** the original FFS corpus was ₹10,000 crore (announced 2016). Secondary/PIB-referenced sources indicate the government has since announced a follow-on **"Fund of Funds 2.0"** with an **additional/renewed ₹10,000 crore corpus** to further mobilize capital for startups (exact incremental vs. cumulative framing — **requires further validation** against the primary PIB release, which could not be fetched directly in this session).
- **AIF eligibility:** AIFs receiving FFS commitments must be registered as **Category I or Category II AIFs** under SEBI (AIF) Regulations, 2012, generally with a minimum fund corpus (commonly cited as ₹50 crore, though the exact figure should be confirmed against SIDBI's current operational guidelines), and are evaluated on manager track record, investment discipline, and operational infrastructure.
- **Matching requirement:** AIFs receiving FFS capital are typically required to **match SIDBI's contribution at a minimum ratio (commonly cited as 2:1 AIF:SIDBI, i.e., SIDBI's share is a minority of the fund)**, and must deploy the resulting capital **exclusively into DPIIT-recognized startups**.
- **Startup eligibility:** a startup cannot apply to FFS/SIDBI directly. It must be **DPIIT-recognized** and must independently secure investment from one of the FFS-backed AIFs whose investment thesis (stage, sector, geography) matches the startup — i.e., **FFS eligibility is really "does an FFS-backed VC fund want to invest in you," gated on DPIIT recognition as a threshold requirement, not a scheme with its own separate application form.**
- **Reported scale (as of cited secondary sources, March 2025):** SIDBI had committed **over ₹11,147 crore across 144 AIFs**, supporting **1,100+ startups**, and mobilizing over ₹20,000 crore of downstream investment.
[PIB press release on FFS 2.0 (referenced via search indexing; not independently fetched — 403)](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2251638&reg=3&lang=1); [PIB press release on FFS deployment figures (referenced via search indexing; not independently fetched — 403)](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=1895964); [SIDBI Fund of Funds page (official; not independently fetched — 403)](https://www.sidbi.in/annualreport/AnnualReport202122/fund-of-funds.php); corroborating secondary summary: [ProjectSarthi](https://projectsarthi.com/schemes/ffs/).

## 5. Startup Haryana (Haryana State Startup Policy)

### 5.1 Governing policy

The current framework is the **Haryana State Startup Policy, 2022**, administered through the **Startup Haryana** portal under the Department of Industries & Commerce, Government of Haryana (and, for the IT-specific dimension, the Department of Information Technology, Electronics & Communication, Haryana).
[Official Startup Haryana portal (not independently fetched — 403)](https://startupharyana.gov.in/); [Official policy page (not independently fetched — 403)](https://startupharyana.gov.in/front/startup-haryana-policy); [Haryana IT Dept. summary page (not independently fetched — 403)](https://haryanait.gov.in/entrepreneur-startup-policy/); [Startup India's own state-policy summary page for Haryana (not independently fetched — 403)](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/state-startup-policies/Haryana-state-policy.html); corroborating secondary summary: [DrishtiIAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/state-pcs-current-affairs/haryana-state-startup-policy-2022).

### 5.2 Eligibility criteria (as reported by secondary sources)

- Must hold **DPIIT recognition** as a starting gate.
- The startup's **registered office must be located in Haryana**.
- Standard DPIIT-style entity/age/turnover tests generally apply as a baseline (Private Limited Company/LLP/Partnership Firm; under ~10 years old; turnover under the applicable cap; engaged in innovation/new business models) — Haryana's scheme rides on top of DPIIT recognition rather than replacing it. **Requires further validation** of whether Haryana has since aligned its own turnover threshold to the new (2026) ₹200 crore DPIIT figure, or still references the older ₹100 crore figure in its own policy text.
[IndiaFilings summary](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/startup-haryana); [Startup India Haryana state page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/state-startup-policies/Haryana-state-policy.html) (not independently fetched — 403).

### 5.3 Incubation support

- Startups admitted to state-supported incubation centres can access **subsidized space and plug-and-play facilities for up to 3 years** (secondary sources note this was extended from a prior 1-year limit under an earlier version of the policy).
[DrishtiIAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/state-pcs-current-affairs/haryana-state-startup-policy-2022); [KIP Financial Consultancy summary of the Acceleration Programs Scheme](https://kipfinancial.com/assistance-in-acceleration-programs-scheme-under-haryana-state-startup-policy-2022/).

### 5.4 Fiscal incentive schemes

Secondary sources describe (at least) the following fiscal incentive schemes under the 2022 policy — **these should be verified for current amounts/caps directly on `startupharyana.gov.in/pages/fiscal-benefits`, which could not be fetched directly in this session:**

| Scheme (as reported) | Reported benefit |
|---|---|
| Seed grant | Up to **₹10 lakh** per eligible DPIIT+Haryana-registered startup |
| Lease rental reimbursement | **30%** reimbursement of lease rental (general startups); **45%** for women-led startups; capped at **₹5 lakh for one year** |
| Patent cost reimbursement | **100%** of filing fees/consultancy costs, up to **₹25 lakh**, for domestic and international patents |
| Net SGST reimbursement | **50% of Net SGST** for **7 years**, capped at **100% of Fixed Capital Investment** |
| Government tender participation | Liberalized eligibility criteria (turnover/experience) for participating in **Haryana state government tenders** |

[IndiaFilings](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/startup-haryana); [Startup Haryana fiscal benefits page (official; not independently fetched — 403)](https://startupharyana.gov.in/pages/fiscal-benefits); Haryana Seed Funding Scheme document referenced at [startupharyana.gov.in PDF (not independently fetched — 403)](https://startupharyana.gov.in/assets/images/pdf/Startup-Seed-Funding-Scheme.pdf).

**Note:** the Net SGST reimbursement in particular is a benefit that is generally structured for entities with meaningful **in-state GST/SGST liability from sales**, which is a consideration relevant to how a SaaS company structures its billing/registration if it wanted to pursue this benefit — this is a factual observation about the mechanism, not a claim about Yorkstn's tax position.

### 5.5 Registration process

- Registration is described as running through the **Startup India DPIIT portal/NSWS first**, followed by a Haryana-specific registration/empanelment step on the **Startup Haryana** web portal (Department of Industries & Commerce) to access the state-specific fiscal benefits.
- No government fee is reported for either the DPIIT or the state-level Startup Haryana registration.
[Startup Haryana portal (not independently fetched — 403)](https://startupharyana.gov.in/); corroborating secondary process summaries: [Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/startup-india-registration-in-haryana); [LegalFidelity](https://www.legalfidelity.com/startup-india-registration/haryana).

## 6. Other potentially relevant national programmes

### 6.1 MeitY Startup Hub — SAMRIDH (Startup Accelerators of MeitY for pRoduct Innovation, Development and growtH)

- Run by the **Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY)** through the **MeitY Startup Hub**, aimed specifically at **software product** startups (the scheme name is explicitly software-product-oriented).
- **Reported eligibility:** DPIIT-recognized; technology-focused; early-stage; scalable and innovative business model; **software-based product**; operations in India.
- **Reported mechanism:** MeitY funds accelerators (not startups directly), who run cohorts (reportedly 5–10 startups per cohort) and can extend **one-to-one matching funding support of up to ₹40 lakh** per startup, alongside acceleration/mentoring services.
- **Reported scale:** 373 startups selected over roughly five years, of which 241 secured matching funding (as of a February 2026-dated news item).
- This is the **most directly on-thesis national programme for a B2B software/SaaS company** among those reviewed, since (unlike PLI, which is hardware-manufacturing-specific — see §6.3) it explicitly targets software products.
[MeitY Startup Hub official SAMRIDH page (not independently fetched — 403)](https://msh.meity.gov.in/schemes/samridh); corroborating secondary summaries: [Razorpay Rize](https://razorpay.com/rize/blogs/samridh-programme); [Corpseed](https://www.corpseed.com/service/samridh-scheme); news item: [TMV.in, Feb 2026](https://tmv.in/article/samridh-scheme-backs-373-startups-241-secure-matching-funding-meity-date=2026-02-05).

### 6.2 Digital India / general MeitY programmes

- "Digital India" is a broad government umbrella initiative/mission rather than a single scheme with defined startup eligibility criteria of its own; it functions more as policy context (e.g., digital infrastructure, e-governance, cybersecurity, digital payments push) under which more specific programmes (like SAMRIDH, GENESIS) sit. **No single, specific "Digital India startup eligibility criterion" was found that would apply distinctly from the schemes already covered above. Requires further validation** if a more targeted current Digital India sub-scheme exists that is relevant to enterprise software companies.
- **MeitY GENESIS (Gen-Next Support for Innovative Startups)** was also referenced in search results as a matching-investment-funding scheme adjacent to SAMRIDH, targeting deep-tech and Tier-2/3 city startups. **Requires further validation** of its precise eligibility criteria and current operational status — this was not independently verified against a primary source in this session.
[YouthNet reference to MeitY GENESIS call for applications](https://www.youthnet.org.in/inviting-application-for-the-meity-genesis-matching-investment-funding-scheme/); official reference point: [MeitY Startup Hub](https://msh.meity.gov.in/) (not independently fetched — 403).

### 6.3 Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes

- PLI schemes (including "PLI 2.0 for IT Hardware," covering laptops, tablets, servers, and similar physical devices) are **manufacturing-incentive schemes tied to incremental domestic production/sales of physical hardware**, with incentives (reported as roughly 5% of net incremental sales over a 6-year window for the IT Hardware PLI 2.0) paid to companies that manufacture qualifying hardware in India.
- **This is very unlikely to be relevant to a B2B SaaS / enterprise-AI software company**, since PLI schemes as currently structured are anchored to physical manufacturing output, not software/services revenue. No PLI scheme covering pure software-as-a-service revenue was identified in this research. **This is a negative finding, not "requires further validation" — the mechanism (incremental manufactured-goods sales) is structurally incompatible with a services/SaaS revenue model as far as could be determined.**

## 7. Alignment analysis for a company like Yorkstn

> **This entire section is analytical inference and opinion, not a factual claim of eligibility.** It has not been validated against Yorkstn's actual cap table, financials, incorporation documents, or a filed DPIIT application, and none of the researchers preparing this document are qualified to render a legal or tax opinion. Any eligibility determination must be made through the actual DPIIT/NSWS/Startup Haryana application processes, ideally with a company-secretary or startup-focused CA/law firm reviewing the specific facts.

Based only on the profile described in the task framing — an early-stage startup building a technology platform to help international consumer brands enter the Indian market, which plausibly functions as a **B2B SaaS / enterprise-software / cross-border-commerce-enablement platform** — the following observations seem directionally plausible, each labeled by criterion:

- **Entity type & age:** If Yorkstn is incorporated in India as a Private Limited Company (the typical structure for a VC-fundable Indian startup) and is within its first 10 years, it would meet the baseline entity-type and age tests for DPIIT recognition (§2.2, §2.3). *Inference — actual incorporation status/date not verified here.*
- **Turnover:** Early-stage startups are very likely to sit comfortably under the ₹200 crore (or even the older ₹100 crore) turnover ceiling; turnover is unlikely to be a binding constraint at this stage. *Inference.*
- **Innovation / scalability narrative:** The stated business — a platform-based, technology-mediated service helping international brands navigate market entry (which plausibly involves things like localization, compliance, logistics/distribution matching, discovery, or similar platform mechanics) — is the kind of narrative that DPIIT examiners and MeitY/SAMRIDH-style programmes generally look favorably on, *if* it is genuinely productized/technology-driven (software, data, or platform IP) rather than a pure consulting/services model with limited repeatability. Whether Yorkstn's actual product satisfies DPIIT's qualitative "innovation or scalable business model" test (§2.5) is a judgment call that depends on specifics (proprietary technology, data assets, automation depth) not available to this research. *Inference — flagged as the single most important open question for an actual DPIIT application.*
- **Not a "reconstruction" of an existing business:** Likely satisfied for a genuinely new startup, but should be confirmed there is no predecessor entity/business being restructured into Yorkstn (§2.6). *Inference.*
- **Employment & economic-impact narrative:** A platform connecting international brands to the Indian market plausibly creates knock-on employment (partner-side operations, customer success, possibly logistics/retail-adjacent roles) beyond direct headcount — this is the kind of "wealth/employment generation" language DPIIT and state schemes reward in their qualitative assessment, but it is a narrative point to substantiate in an application, not an automatic qualifier. *Inference.*
- **IP opportunities:** If Yorkstn's platform involves proprietary software (matching/recommendation logic, compliance-automation tooling, data infrastructure, or similar), there may be patent/trademark assets worth protecting — historically via the SIPP fee-rebate scheme (§3.5). **However, SIPP is reported to have lapsed as of 31 March 2026 with no confirmed renewal** — so any IP-filing cost planning should currently assume **no government fee subsidy is available** unless/until this is confirmed otherwise on `ipindia.gov.in`. This is a concrete, time-sensitive planning point worth flagging internally.
- **Angel tax:** Now moot for capital-raising purposes generally, since Section 56(2)(viib) was abolished for all companies from AY2025-26 (§3.2) — Yorkstn (like any Indian company) should not need DPIIT-specific angel-tax immunity for current/future fundraising rounds, though it could still matter for any pre-FY2025-26 share issuances still under assessment. *Inference, with the abolition itself being a verified fact.*
- **Section 80-IAC tax holiday:** Potentially attractive once Yorkstn has taxable profits (which is often not the case in the earliest stages of a venture-backed startup, since 80-IAC exempts *profits*, not revenue, and many early-stage startups are pre-profit). Worth revisiting once/if Yorkstn approaches profitability, noting the **separate IMB application requirement** beyond bare DPIIT recognition (§3.1). *Inference.*
- **Public procurement / GeM:** Likely low near-term relevance unless Yorkstn's go-to-market includes selling directly to Indian government departments or PSUs (uncommon for a brand-market-entry platform, whose customers are presumably international consumer brands and/or Indian retail/distribution partners, not government buyers) — but flagged as a benefit that costs nothing to register for if a future government-adjacent customer segment emerges. *Inference.*
- **SISFS:** The ₹20 lakh grant ceiling and requirement to route through a DPIIT-empanelled incubator, combined with the "incorporated not more than 2 years ago" condition (§4.1), make this most relevant only in a narrow early window post-incorporation — if Yorkstn is past that 2-year mark, SISFS itself would no longer be an available pathway, though the qualitative "seed-stage validation" framing may still describe Yorkstn's stage narratively. *Inference — actual incorporation date not verified here.*
- **FFS (Fund of Funds):** Not something Yorkstn could apply to directly in any case (§4.2) — relevant only insofar as some of Yorkstn's prospective VC investors might themselves be FFS-backed AIFs, which is a fact about the investor's fund structure, not about Yorkstn. *Inference.*
- **Startup Haryana:** Directly relevant **only if Yorkstn's registered office is in Haryana** (§5.2) — this is a factual, checkable condition (not an inference) that determines whether the entire state-scheme layer (seed grant, lease reimbursement, patent-cost reimbursement, SGST reimbursement) is even reachable. If Yorkstn's registered office is not in Haryana, none of §5 applies regardless of any other alignment. *This is the single highest-leverage fact to confirm before pursuing anything in §5.*
- **SAMRIDH (MeitY):** Of the national programmes reviewed, this looks like the best-fitting non-DPIIT-baseline national scheme *if* Yorkstn's platform is genuinely software-product-centric (as opposed to services-heavy) — worth deeper investigation given the explicit "software product startup" framing of the scheme (§6.1). *Inference.*
- **PLI:** Not relevant — Yorkstn is a software/services platform, not a physical-goods manufacturer, and current PLI schemes are structurally tied to manufactured-hardware sales (§6.3). This is close to a factual negative finding rather than mere inference, though it rests on the assumption that Yorkstn does not also manufacture qualifying hardware, which was not independently confirmed.

### Open compliance questions worth resolving before any application

1. What is Yorkstn's exact incorporation date, entity type, and registered-office state? (Gates almost every eligibility test above.)
2. Has Yorkstn crossed the 2-year mark (relevant to SISFS eligibility) or the 10-year mark (relevant to baseline DPIIT/80-IAC eligibility)?
3. Is Yorkstn currently profitable, loss-making, or pre-revenue? (Determines whether 80-IAC is currently useful vs. a future-facing benefit.)
4. Does Yorkstn's product involve patentable/trademarkable technology it would want to file for, and if so, on what timeline — given SIPP's lapsed status, should IP budget assume full-cost filing for now?
5. Does Yorkstn's capital structure (aggregate paid-up capital + premium, promoter shareholding %) fall within any scheme-specific caps still in force (e.g., the historical ₹25 crore angel-tax-immunity cap, now largely moot; the SISFS 51% Indian-promoter-shareholding condition, which may or may not be met depending on cap table/investor composition)?
6. Has Yorkstn received any prior monetary support from a Central/State government scheme that might interact with the SISFS "≤₹10 lakh prior support" condition?
