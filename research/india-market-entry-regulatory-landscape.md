## 1. Scope, Methodology, and Disclaimer

### Scope
This document surveys the operational and regulatory landscape that an international consumer brand typically encounters when entering the Indian market: legal entity formation, import/customs, tax (GST), product compliance (BIS, Legal Metrology), IP registration, FDI policy (including retail and e-commerce), warehousing, labour/payroll compliance, manufacturing, commercial real estate, franchising, distribution, consumer/regional diversity, state-level variation, government incentives, and digital commerce rules.

### Methodology
Research was conducted via web search (Claude's WebSearch tool) on 2026-07-23. **Direct WebFetch access to primary government sources (mca.gov.in, dgft.gov.in, cbic.gov.in, rbi.org.in, bis.gov.in, ipindia.gov.in, investindia.gov.in, gst.gov.in, pib.gov.in) and even neutral test pages was unavailable in this research session — every fetch attempt returned an HTTP 403 regardless of domain.** As a result, this document relies on:
1. **Official government URLs** cited as the authoritative reference for a rule/scheme (identified via search results, titles, and snippets that quote or summarize them), even though the underlying page content could not be independently re-verified by direct fetch in this session; and
2. **Reputable secondary sources** — law firm client alerts, Big 4/advisory market-entry guides, and specialist compliance publishers — whose search-result summaries are quoted or paraphrased to substantiate specific facts, thresholds, and procedures.

Because primary-source text could not be directly pulled and re-read in full, **every reader should independently verify current rules directly on the cited official site before relying on any figure, threshold, fee, or timeline in this document** — especially anything that looks like it could have changed since early-to-mid 2026 (India's FDI policy, GST slabs, and labour codes have all seen active change in this period, per the sources below).

### Disclaimer
This document is a research reference for internal product/strategy use. **It is not legal, tax, or regulatory advice.** Indian regulations (FEMA/RBI rules, FDI policy, GST rates, labour codes, BIS orders) change frequently and are subject to central and state-level variation. Any commercial decision must be validated with qualified Indian counsel, a chartered accountant, and/or a company secretary, and against the live text of the primary source. ## 2. Business Registration & Legal Entity Formation for Foreign Companies

Foreign companies have several established routes to establish a presence in India, per Invest India (the national investment promotion agency) and the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA):

- **Wholly Owned Subsidiary (WOS) or Joint Venture (JV)** — incorporated as a Private or Public Limited Company under the **Companies Act, 2013**, via MCA's **SPICe+ (Part A and Part B)** integrated web form along with eMoA/eAoA. This is the most common route for a brand wanting full operating flexibility (retail, e-commerce, manufacturing, distribution) since it is a domestic Indian company (majority or fully foreign-owned) rather than a mere extension of the foreign parent. [Invest India — Setting Up Business FAQs](https://www.investindia.gov.in/faqs/setting-up-business-in-india); [MCA — Incorporation](https://www.mca.gov.in/MinistryV2/incorporation_company.html)
- **Liaison Office (LO) / Representative Office** — cannot undertake any commercial, trading, manufacturing, or income-generating activity; used purely for communication/market-scoping, and all local expenses must be funded by inward remittance from the foreign parent. Requires RBI/AD-Category-I-bank approval under FEMA. [RBI FAQ — Liaison/Branch/Project Offices](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/english/scripts/FAQs.aspx?Id=1303); [Invest India FAQs](https://www.investindia.gov.in/faqs/setting-up-business-in-india)
- **Branch Office (BO)** — unlike an LO, a BO can generate revenue, invoice, and contract in India, but is still legally an extension of the foreign parent (not a separate Indian entity) and its permitted activities are constrained by RBI approval terms. [RBI FAQ](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/english/scripts/FAQs.aspx?Id=1303); [India Briefing — Branch Office 2026](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/how-to-set-up-branch-office-india-2026-44862.html/)
- **Project Office (PO)** — permitted where a foreign company has secured a specific contract from an Indian entity to execute a project in India; temporary/project-scoped. [Invest India FAQs](https://www.investindia.gov.in/faqs/setting-up-business-in-india)
- **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)** — foreign companies/nationals may set up an LLP under the **LLP Act, 2008**, subject to sectoral FDI conditions and the requirement that at least one designated partner be a resident of India. [Invest India FAQs](https://www.investindia.gov.in/faqs/setting-up-business-in-india)

**Regulatory filing:** Any foreign company establishing an LO/BO/PO must file **eForm FC-1** with the Registrar of Companies (ROC) within 30 days of establishing a place of business in India, under the **Companies (Registration of Foreign Companies) Rules, 2014**, accompanied by RBI/FEMA approval (or a declaration that no such approval is required). [MCA / ROC guidance summarized by KSandK](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/mca-foreign-companies-operating-in-india/); [Beacon Filing — Form FC-1](https://beaconfiling.com/glossary/fc-1-foreign-company-registration)

**Governing regulation for LO/BO/PO:** Foreign Exchange Management (Establishment in India of a branch office or a liaison office or a project office or any other place of business) Regulations, 2016, issued by RBI on 31 March 2016. **RBI issued draft reforms to this framework reported in 2025/2026** — the exact current terms should be checked directly against RBI's latest master direction/circular. [India Briefing — Branch Office 2026 draft reforms](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/how-to-set-up-branch-office-india-2026-44862.html/); [RBI FAQ](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/english/scripts/FAQs.aspx?Id=1303)

**Practical implication for a consumer brand:** A WOS/JV private limited company is generally the vehicle of choice for brands intending to actually trade, retail, or manufacture in India (as opposed to LO, which is representative-only). *Requires further validation with Indian counsel for the specific brand's operating model (retail vs. wholesale vs. e-commerce vs. manufacturing) since sectoral FDI conditions differ (see Section 7).*

## 3. Import Procedures, Customs Duties, and DGFT/CBIC Processes

### Importer Exporter Code (IEC)
- Any individual/company engaged in import or export of goods or services must obtain a 10-digit **Importer Exporter Code (IEC)** from the **Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT)**, under the **Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act, 1992**. [DGFT — dgft.gov.in]; secondary sourcing: [Vakilsearch — IEC 2026 guide](https://vakilsearch.com/article/iec-import-export-code-registration-india-2026/); [Razorpay — IEC guide](https://razorpay.com/blog/how-to-apply-for-an-iec-code-online-india/)
- Application is filed online via **Form ANF-2A** on the DGFT portal, linked to PAN, with a filing fee reported as ₹500 and typical issuance within 1–3 business days (**Requires further validation** — fee/timeline figures came from secondary aggregator sites, not a direct DGFT fetch). [Vakilsearch](https://vakilsearch.com/article/iec-import-export-code-registration-india-2026/)
- IEC has lifetime validity but requires an **annual update** (confirmation of details) each April–June; failure to update can lead to deactivation — this annual-update rule was introduced in 2021. [Targol Legal — IEC 2026 guide](https://targolegal.com/blog/import-export-code-iec-2026-who-needs-it-how-to-apply-on-dgft-the-annual-update-rule-2/)

### Customs clearance (CBIC)
- Import clearance is administered by the **Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC)** under the **Customs Act, 1962**. Documentation is processed largely through **ICEGATE** (customs EDI gateway) and **e-SANCHIT** (digitized document upload for licenses/permits). [Blogs.tarangya — Customs clearance & ICEGATE guide](https://blogs.tarangya.com/complete-guide-to-customs-clearance-in-india/); [CBIC circular summary — newsonair](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/cbic-introduces-deferred-customs-duty-payment-facility-for-eligible-manufacturer-importers)
- **Recent 2026 developments** reported: CBIC Circular No. 06/2026-Customs (Feb 2026) introduced **Auto Goods Registration** and expanded **Auto Out-of-Charge (OOC)** for eligible importers, moving toward a "faceless" clearance model; and a new **deferred customs duty payment facility** for "Eligible Manufacturer Importers" (EMIs) effective 1 April 2026–31 March 2028, letting eligible manufacturers clear goods without paying duty at the border and instead pay monthly (applications via the AEO portal, aeoindia.gov.in, from 1 March 2026). [PIB press release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2234116&reg=3&lang=1); [TaxGuru summary](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/deferred-customs-duty-facility-extended-eligible-manufacturer-importers-expedite-clearance.html); [India Briefing — EMI scheme](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/cbic-deferred-customs-duty-emi-scheme-explained-43202.html/)
- **These are very recent (2026) changes; given the July 2026 "as of" date of this research and the pace of CBIC circular activity, all specifics (thresholds, effective dates, eligible categories) should be re-verified against the live CBIC circular text at cbic.gov.in before being relied upon.**

### Customs duty structure
- Import duty in India typically comprises Basic Customs Duty (BCD), plus IGST (Integrated GST) levied at the point of import, and in some cases a Social Welfare Surcharge — exact rates are product (HSN-code) specific. *Requires further validation with a customs broker / CBIC tariff schedule for any specific product category a brand intends to import, as duty rates vary widely by HSN classification and change via Finance Bill / customs notifications.*

### Warehousing under customs law (bonded warehousing)
- Under **Chapter IX of the Customs Act, 1962**, importers can use **public bonded warehouses** (e.g., operated by the Central Warehousing Corporation) or obtain a **private bonded warehouse license** (Section 58) to store imported goods without paying duty upfront — duty is deferred until goods are cleared for home consumption, generally up to one year from bonding (subject to conditions). **Special warehouses (Section 58A)** exist for high-risk/sensitive goods (e.g., chemicals, tobacco). [TaxGuru — Private Bonded Warehouse](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/private-customs-bonded-warehouse.html); [Bhatt & Joshi Associates — Customs Bonded Warehouses legal framework](https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/customs-bonded-warehouse/); [Logistics Insider](https://www.logisticsinsider.in/the-scope-of-customs-bonded-warehousing-in-india/)

## 4. GST Registration and Compliance for Foreign Entities

- India's indirect tax is **Goods and Services Tax (GST)**, administered jointly by the Centre (CGST/IGST) and states (SGST), via the **GST Network / gst.gov.in** portal.
- **General registration threshold:** mandatory GST registration applies once aggregate turnover crosses **₹40 lakh for goods** / **₹20 lakh for services** in most states (lower thresholds, ₹20 lakh goods / ₹10 lakh services, apply in specified special-category states). A **Composition Scheme** (simplified, lower flat-rate tax, no input tax credit) is available for smaller goods suppliers up to ₹1.5 crore turnover (₹75 lakh in special-category states) and ₹50 lakh for qualifying service providers/mixed suppliers, at reduced rates. [ClearTax — GST registration threshold](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-registration-limits-increased); [IndiaFilings — GST threshold](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/what-is-the-minimum-turnover-for-gst)
- **Foreign entities without a fixed place of business in India** that occasionally supply goods/services in India must register as a **Non-Resident Taxable Person (NRTP)** — via **Form GST REG-09** (mirroring GST REG-01 for resident taxpayers) — and this registration must be obtained **at least 5 days before commencing business**, requires an **authorised representative in India** with valid PAN, and requires filing **GSTR-5** monthly returns. [GST portal tutorial reference](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/registration/Registration_by_Non-resident_Taxable_Person.htm); [IndiaFilings — NRTP registration](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/gst-registration-non-resident-taxable-person)
- A brand operating through an Indian WOS (Section 2), by contrast, registers as a normal domestic taxpayer against the WOS's PAN — the NRTP route is specifically for entities without a local fixed establishment (rare for a brand with real retail/distribution operations).
- **GST rate structure ("GST 2.0"):** Effective **22 September 2025**, India simplified GST into primarily **0%, 5%, 18%, and 40%** slabs (down from the earlier multi-slab structure); everyday consumer items like packaged food/toothpaste/soap are often at 5%, most consumer electronics/general goods at 18%, and luxury/"sin" goods (premium cars, tobacco) at 40%. [ClearTax — GST rates 2026](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-rates); [Bajaj Finance — GST rates 2026](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/gst-rates-in-india)
- **HSN code reporting** on invoices/GSTR-1 is mandatory, with digit-length requirements scaling by turnover (no mandatory HSN below ₹1.5 crore turnover; 2-digit between ₹1.5–5 crore; 4-digit above ₹5 crore). [Avalara — HSN codes for GST](https://www.avalara.com/in/en/resources/whitepapers/hsn-codes-goods-service-tax-gst-india.html); [Tax2win — HSN guide](https://tax2win.in/guide/gst-hsn-code)
- **Multi-state operations:** GST registration is required **state-by-state** wherever a business has a "fixed establishment" (e.g., a warehouse) — inventory stored and sold from a warehouse in another state generally triggers a separate GSTR registration in that state. **E-Way Bills** are mandatory for inter-state movement of goods valued over ₹50,000 (Rule 138, CGST Rules 2017), with validity generally of 1 day per 200 km of transport; intra-state thresholds vary by state. [Tally Solutions — Multi-state tax compliance](https://tallysolutions.com/smart-moves/tax-and-compliance/multi-state-tax-compliance-india/); [ClearTax — E-way bill rules](https://cleartax.in/s/eway-bill-gst-rules-compliance)

*Given GST 2.0's very recent (Sept 2025) rate restructuring, all specific rates cited above should be checked against the live GST rate notification / gst.gov.in before use in any pricing or compliance model.*

## 5. BIS (Bureau of Indian Standards) Certification Requirements

- **BIS**, established under the **BIS Act, 2016**, is India's national standards body and administers mandatory product certification via **Quality Control Orders (QCOs)**.
- Products falling under a QCO **cannot be imported, sold, or distributed in India** unless they carry the requisite **ISI mark**/BIS certification — reported to now cover **over 679 product categories** spanning electronics, chemicals, machinery, and various consumer goods (this breadth figure is from a secondary compliance-consultancy source and should be checked against the current BIS QCO list). [China Briefing / India Briefing — BIS certification guide](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/a-guide-to-bis-certification-in-india-for-foreign-manufacturers-37916.html/)
- **Foreign Manufacturers Certification Scheme (FMCS):** the primary route for overseas manufacturers of ISI-marked products, requiring appointment of an **Authorized Indian Representative (AIR)** and, per secondary sources, a **Performance Bank Guarantee (PBG)** reportedly around **USD 10,000** — *this fee figure requires further validation directly against BIS's current FMCS fee schedule.* [Mondaq — BIS Compliance FMCS/Scheme X](https://www.mondaq.com/india/compliance/1674006/bis-compliance-in-india-navigating-fmcs-and-scheme-x); [Comercinate — FMCS process/cost/time](https://comercinate.com/bis-fmcs-process-cost-time-validity/)
- **Scheme X** — a newer BIS conformity route, reported to be machinery-focused, coming into force for a wide range of equipment from **28 August 2025**, with reported extension activity through **September 2026** under an "omnibus technical regulation order 2024." *Given how recent and fluid this is, verify current applicability directly with BIS (bis.gov.in) for any specific product.* [Legal500 — FMCS developments](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/foreign-manufactures-certification-scheme/); [ASC Group — BIS CRS 2026 updates](https://ascgroupconsultant.wordpress.com/2026/01/16/bis-crs-certification-2026-compulsory-registration-scheme-enhancements-and-circulars/)
- **Compulsory Registration Scheme (CRS):** applies specifically to electronics/IT/telecom products under the **Electronics and IT Goods (Requirements for Compulsory Registration) Order**; product coverage reportedly expanded from an initial ~15 categories to over 76+ (smartwatches, laptops, LED lights, printers, CCTV cameras, batteries, Bluetooth speakers/headphones, etc.). [REACH24H — BIS CRS practical guide](https://en.reach24h.com/news/insights/chemical/bis-crs-compulsory-registration-scheme); [eStartIndia — BIS CRS 2026 update](https://www.estartindia.com/knowledge-hub/blog/bis-crs-update-2026-new-rules-for-electronics-manufacturers-in-india)

**Practical implication:** Any consumer brand importing electronics, certain appliances, toys, or specified chemical/personal-care categories into India should check the current BIS QCO list for their exact product/HSN code *before* committing to an import plan — lead time and cost of FMCS/CRS certification can materially affect launch timelines.

## 6. Labelling Requirements (Legal Metrology)

- Retail packaged goods sold in India are governed by the **Legal Metrology Act, 2009** and the **Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 (LMPC Rules)**, administered by the Department of Consumer Affairs / state Legal Metrology departments. [Legal Metrology Rules text (Central Warehousing Corporation host)](https://cwnm.nic.in/LMPC-Rule2011.pdf); [SS Rana — Retail Package Labeling](https://ssrana.in/articles/labelling-on-retail-packages/)
- **Mandatory label declarations** on retail packages include: name and address of the manufacturer/packer/importer, common/generic name of the commodity, net quantity, month/year of manufacture or packing, Maximum Retail Price (MRP) inclusive of all taxes, and consumer-care contact details. [TigerPug — BIS/Legal Metrology/Labeling guide](https://www.tigerpug.com/post/understanding-bis-legal-metrology-and-labeling-requirements-for-imported-products-in-india)
- **For imported products specifically**, the label must additionally state the **importer's name and address** and the **country of origin/manufacture/assembly**. An **LMPC registration/certificate** is typically required before an importer can bring packaged goods labeled to Indian requirements into the country. [TaxTMI — Labeling of goods imported into India](https://www.taxtmi.com/manuals?id=7232); [Diligence Certification — LMPC certificate guide](https://www.diligencecertification.com/lmpc-certificate/)
- Non-compliance can result in fines, imprisonment, and seizure of non-compliant stock. [Zeus Law Associates — Legal Metrology packaging/labelling requirements](https://zeus.firm.in/legal-metrology-law-requirements-for-packaging-labelling-of-goods-in-india/)

**Practical implication:** International brands typically cannot simply ship globally-labeled packaging into India — compliant labels (or supplementary stickers, subject to LMPC conditions) must be applied, and MRP-inclusive-of-tax pricing display is a distinctly Indian requirement not common in many source markets.

## 7. Trademark and Patent Registration (IP India / CGPDTM)

### Trademarks
- Governed by the **Trade Marks Act, 1999**; administered by the **Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (CGPDTM)**, under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, with registries in Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, and Ahmedabad. [ebizfiling — IP India trademark registration guide](https://ebizfiling.com/blog/ipindiaonline-trademark-registration-guide-2025/); [Harris Sliwoski — Registering a Trademark in India](https://harris-sliwoski.com/blog/registering-a-trademark-in-india-what-international-businesses-need-to-know/)
- **Foreign applicants** must appoint a local trademark agent/attorney and provide a notarized/apostilled **Power of Attorney**. Process: prior trademark search → application filing (with goods/services class and any priority claim) → examination → publication in the Trademark Journal → a **4-month opposition window** for third parties → registration. [IPLINK Asia — Trademark registration process](https://www.iplink-asia.com/article-detail.php?id=1232)
- **Practical note:** given India's status as a Paris Convention/Madrid Protocol member, brands can often claim priority from a home-country filing or file via the Madrid System designating India — *requires further validation of Madrid Protocol procedural specifics with IP counsel for the brand's specific home jurisdiction.*

### Patents
- Governed by the **Patents Act, 1970**; also administered by CGPDTM. Foreign applicants can file directly (convention application) or via **PCT national phase entry**, and (like trademarks) do not face discrimination based on nationality, but must provide an **address for service in India**. [WIPO — Patent System of India](https://www.wipo.int/patent-judicial-guide/en/full-guide/india); [Global Patent Filing — Foreigners filing patents in India](https://www.globalpatentfiling.com/blog/Can-Foreign-Nationals-Register-Patents-in-India-A-Comprehensive-Guide-for-PCT-Applicants)
- Applications are published 18 months after filing/priority date (whichever is earlier). A **Foreign Filing License (FFL)** is required before an Indian-origin invention can be filed abroad first; reportedly takes ~21 days to obtain, or filing abroad becomes permissible without an explicit FFL if six weeks pass after the Indian filing without a secrecy direction. *(Requires further validation — FFL specifics are more relevant to Indian-origin inventions than to an international brand's home-country IP, so likely low-relevance for most Yorkstn use cases, but flagged for completeness.)* [Global Patent Filing](https://www.globalpatentfiling.com/blog/Can-Foreign-Nationals-Register-Patents-in-India-A-Comprehensive-Guide-for-PCT-Applicants)

**Practical implication for consumer brands:** Trademark protection (brand name, logo) is the IP workstream most relevant to a consumer brand's India entry, and should generally be initiated **well before** commercial launch or franchise/distribution deals, given the multi-month opposition and examination timeline.

## 8. Retail Sector FDI Regulations (Single-Brand vs. Multi-Brand)

Policy is set by the **Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT)**, Ministry of Commerce and Industry, in India's Consolidated FDI Policy.

### Single-Brand Retail Trading (SBRT)
- **100% FDI permitted under the automatic route** (no prior government approval needed). [Invest India — Sectoral FAQs Retail & E-commerce](https://www.investindia.gov.in/faq-pdf/86/en); [ClearTax — Single Brand Retail Trading](https://cleartax.in/s/single-brand-retail-trading)
- Where FDI exceeds 51%, a **mandatory 30% local sourcing requirement** applies, which can be met as a 5-year average initially and annually thereafter. [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/single-brand-retail-trading); [Beacon Filing — SBRT vs MBRT](https://beaconfiling.com/comparisons/single-brand-vs-multi-brand-retail-fdi)
- Reported policy liberalizations over time have allowed SBRT entities to (a) sell online without first establishing a physical store, and (b) count sourcing from India toward the 30% requirement even for goods that are exported or used for global operations. [India Briefing — FDI reforms single brand retail](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-fdi-reforms-single-brand-retails-contract-manufacturing-19132.html/); [UNCTAD Investment Policy Hub — India SEZ sourcing](https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/investment-policy-monitor/measures/3516/india-foreign-single-brand-retailers-can-source-from-sezs-to-satisfy-fdi-requirements)

### Multi-Brand Retail Trading (MBRT)
- FDI is capped at **51%** and requires **government approval** (not automatic route). [PIB — FDI Policy in Multi Brand Retail Trading](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=191486)
- Minimum FDI inflow of **USD 100 million**, with **at least 50% of FDI required to be invested in "back-end infrastructure"** (processing, manufacturing, distribution — excluding land cost/rentals) within 3 years. [CMS Law — India multi-brand retailers](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/india-opens-up-to-foreign-ownership-of-multi-brand-retailers); [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=191486)
- Retail outlets may only be opened in **cities with population over 1 million** (per the 2011 census), and **individual state governments retain the right to opt in or out** of permitting MBRT within their territory. [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=191486)
- MBRT entities with FDI are **prohibited from e-commerce sales**. [ClearTax / testbook synthesis](https://testbook.com/question-answer/fdi-in-multi-brand-retailing-in-india-is--607845e2f511f7f5ad41a43a)
- **Note on currency of this section:** search results returned some inconsistent/ambiguous signals about whether MBRT policy has been further liberalized in 2026 (one secondary source referenced a "2026 reforms" framing while describing what appears to be the long-standing 2012-era policy terms). **This is flagged explicitly as uncertain — verify the current MBRT cap and conditions directly against DPIIT's live Consolidated FDI Policy / press notes before relying on any specific percentage or threshold.**

### Royalty / brand-fee remittance (relevant to franchising and licensing)
- Since **16 December 2009**, India permits royalty, lump-sum technology-transfer fees, and trademark/brand-name usage payments to foreign collaborators **without a cap, on the automatic route** (previously capped at 5% domestic sales / 8% exports for royalty with tech transfer, or 1–2% for trademark-only use). [IndiaCorpLaw — Foreign Collaboration and Royalty Payments](https://indiacorplaw.in/2009/12/18/foreign-collaboration-and-royalty/); [India Briefing — Indian Government Scraps Royalty Limits](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/indian-government-scraps-royalty-limits-1714.html/)

## 9. Warehousing Requirements and Options

- **Customs bonded warehousing** (see Section 3) allows duty deferral for imported stock — relevant for brands importing finished goods before distribution.
- **General (non-customs) commercial warehousing** and **third-party logistics (3PL)** are a large and growing market: India's 3PL market is variously estimated at **USD ~24.9–38.7 billion in 2025**, growing toward **USD ~50–78 billion by the early-to-mid 2030s** across different forecasts (estimates vary meaningfully by research house — treat as directional, not precise). [Mordor Intelligence — India 3PL market](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-3pl-market); [IMARC — India 3PL market](https://www.imarcgroup.com/india-third-party-logistics-market); [Expert Market Research — India 3PL](https://www.expertmarketresearch.com/reports/india-third-party-logistics-3pl-market)
- Growth is driven by e-commerce, **quick commerce** dark-store networks, and PLI-linked manufacturing growth. [Nexdigm — India 3PL industry insight](https://www.nexdigm.com/market-research/insights/blog/india-3pl-industry/)
- **Quick commerce infrastructure** (relevant to a brand's last-mile distribution strategy): as of reported March 2026 data, India had roughly **4,081 operational dark stores** across major platforms (Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart, plus newer entrants Amazon Now, Flipkart Minutes, BigBasket's BB Now) spanning 408 cities in 26 states; Blinkit led with ~46% share. [GlobeNewswire — India Quick Commerce Report 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/20/3277255/28124/en/india-quick-commerce-report-2026-market-to-reach-12-97-billion-by-2029-blinkit-zepto-and-swiggy-instamart-lead-surge-as-jiomart-and-bigbasket-scale-competitive-entry.html); [QuickCommerceMap — India dark store map 2026](https://quickcommercemap.com/reports/india-quick-commerce-map-2026)
- **Multi-state GST implications for warehousing:** storing and selling goods from a warehouse in a state other than the entity's home state generally requires a **separate GST registration in that state** as a "fixed establishment," and **inter-state movement above ₹50,000 in value requires an E-Way Bill**. (See Section 4.) [Tally Solutions — Multi-state tax compliance](https://tallysolutions.com/smart-moves/tax-and-compliance/multi-state-tax-compliance-india/)

## 10. Hiring and Payroll Compliance (Labour Codes, EPFO, ESIC)

- India consolidated **29 central labour laws into 4 Labour Codes** — on Wages, Industrial Relations, Social Security, and Occupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions — which came into force on **21 November 2025**. As of the codes taking effect, **many states had reportedly not yet notified their own implementing rules**, meaning practical compliance can still vary by state even under the unified codes. [WiseMonk — Labor Laws in India 2026 guide](https://www.wisemonk.io/blogs/labor-employment-law-in-india); [LoopHealth — India Labour Codes 2026 HR checklist](https://www.loophealth.com/post/what-indias-new-labour-codes-mean-for-hr-a-complete-compliance-checklist-for-2026)
- **EPFO (Employees' Provident Fund Organisation):** mandatory registration for factories/establishments employing **20 or more persons** (in scheduled industries); this 20-employee threshold has reportedly **not changed** under the new codes per an EPFO clarification. New employees must be enrolled within **30 days** of joining. Reported employer contribution is **12% of wages** (matched by employee 12%), though this can vary by wage ceiling and scheme rules. Establishments below the threshold can register voluntarily. Coverage, once triggered, continues even if headcount later drops below 20. [EPFO — official PIB clarification on 20-employee threshold](https://www.epfindia.gov.in/site_docs/PDFs/Updates/PIB_EPFAct.pdf); [Vakilsearch — EPF Employer Registration 2026](https://vakilsearch.com/article/epf-employer-registration-india-2026/)
- **ESIC (Employees' State Insurance Corporation):** mandatory for factories with **10 or more workers**, and for notified categories of shops/commercial establishments (hotels, restaurants, cinemas, road transport, etc.) with 10+ employees — **though some states apply a 20-employee threshold instead**, so this is state-variable. Reported contribution rates (in effect since July 2019): **employer 3.25%**, **employee 0.75%** of wages. Wage ceiling for ESI coverage is reported at **₹21,000/month** gross (₹25,000/month for persons with disabilities). Registration must be completed within **15 days** of crossing the threshold. [ESIC official contribution page](https://esic.gov.in/contribution); [Vakilsearch — ESI Registration 2026](https://vakilsearch.com/article/esi-registration-india-employer-2026/)
- **Gig/platform workers:** under the new Social Security Code, platform aggregators are reported to be required to contribute **1–2% of turnover (capped at 5% of payments to gig workers)** toward a social security fund — highly relevant to any brand relying on gig-based delivery/quick-commerce partners. **Requires further validation** as this is a new and still-settling area of the code. [LoopHealth — Labour Codes 2026 checklist](https://www.loophealth.com/post/what-indias-new-labour-codes-mean-for-hr-a-complete-compliance-checklist-for-2026)
- **State-level "Shops and Establishments" registration** (in addition to central labour law) is required in essentially every state for any commercial premises (retail store, office, warehouse) — see Section 13 for state examples (Maharashtra "Gumasta" license, Karnataka Shops & Commercial Establishments Act).

*Because the four Labour Codes only took effect in November 2025 and state-level rule notification was reportedly still catching up as of the codes' commencement, treat all thresholds/rates in this section as subject to near-term change and verify against EPFO (epfindia.gov.in), ESIC (esic.gov.in), and the relevant state labour department before use.*

The Indian cosmetics contract-manufacturing market alone was reported to have crossed **₹12,000 crore in 2023**. [IMARC Engineering — Contract Manufacturing in India guide](https://www.imarcengineering.com/blog/contract-manufacturing-in-india); [Jack Cosmetics — Third Party Cosmetic Manufacturing](https://jackcosmetics.in/insights/third-party-cosmetic-manufacturing-business-growth-advantages/)

## 12. Mall Leasing / Commercial Real Estate Practices for Retail Expansion

- Retail leases in Indian malls commonly combine a **fixed "Minimum Guarantee" (MG) rent** with a **Percentage/Revenue-Share rent** component above a turnover threshold — the tenant typically pays **whichever is higher** of the MG or the percentage-of-turnover figure. [Orion Malls — Retail Leasing Models: Fixed Rent vs Revenue Share](https://www.orionmalls.com/blogs/retail-leasing-models-fixed-rent-vs-revenue-sharing)
- **Common Area Maintenance (CAM) charges** — covering security, HVAC, cleaning, parking, common facilities — are billed separately and reported to add roughly **15–25% on top of monthly rent**; tenants are advised to request an itemized CAM breakdown. [Fulin Space — Best practices for retail lease in India](https://fulinspace.com/best-practices-for-retail-lease-in-india/); [MyndSolution — Managing CAM charges](https://www.myndsolution.com/best-practices/managing-cam-charges-and-reconciliation-in-lease-management-in-india/)
- Other standard lease terms to negotiate: **lock-in period**, tenure/escalations, marketing/common promotion charges, stamp duty (a state-level cost on lease registration), termination clauses, and **security deposit** — reported to typically run 2–6 months' rent, with 6 months cited as the cap suggested under the (non-binding, state-adoption-dependent) **Model Tenancy Act**. [CRC Group — Commercial property leasing trends](https://www.crcgroup.in/growing-trend-commercial-property-leasing-india/); [Ginesys — Mall vs High Street Retail](https://www.ginesys.in/blog/mall-vs-high-street-retail-in-india)
- **Mall vs. high-street retail** is a recurring strategic choice for brands — malls offer curated footfall and CAM-managed common infrastructure at a revenue-share cost; high-street stores can have lower occupancy cost but require the brand to manage more operationally itself. [Ginesys — Mall vs High Street Retail in India](https://www.ginesys.in/blog/mall-vs-high-street-retail-in-india)

*Lease terms are negotiated bilaterally and vary widely by mall operator, city, and category — the figures above are illustrative market practice reported by real-estate advisory/blog sources, not statutory requirements, and should be treated as directional only.*

## 13. Franchise Expansion — Legal/Practical Considerations

- **India has no dedicated franchise law.** Franchising operates under a patchwork of general laws: the **Indian Contract Act, 1872**, the **Specific Relief Act**, the **Consumer Protection Act, 2019**, **FEMA/FDI regulations** (for royalty/franchise-fee remittance and equity structuring), IP law (for trademark licensing), and the **Arbitration and Conciliation Act** (commonly used for dispute resolution given no fast-track litigation system). [Altacit — Franchise Law in India](https://www.altacit.com/franchise-law-in-india-legal-framework-for-franchisors-franchisees-2026/); [KSandK — Franchising Laws and Regulations in India](https://ksandk.com/startups/franchising-laws-and-regulations-in-india/); [Law.asia — Franchise law in India: bridging legal gaps](https://law.asia/franchise-law-india-legal-framework-policy-reform/)
- **There is no franchise regulator, no mandatory disclosure regime (unlike, e.g., the US FTC Franchise Rule), and no code of ethics prescribed under Indian law** for franchising — meaning franchise agreements carry unusually heavy weight as the governing document, and both franchisor and franchisee can be held **jointly liable under the Consumer Protection Act** for service quality/misleading-claims/product-deficiency issues. [Law.asia](https://law.asia/franchise-law-india-legal-framework-policy-reform/); [Mondaq — Franchising Comparative Guide India](https://www.mondaq.com/india/corporatecommercial-law/1501728/franchising-comparative-guide)
- **FDI/FEMA compliance is layered on top:** international franchisors must structure royalty payments, franchise fees, and any equity component in the local franchisee entity to comply with sector-specific FDI caps (especially relevant where the underlying business is retail — see Section 8) and FEMA repatriation rules. Since Dec 2009, royalty/brand-fee payments themselves are uncapped under the automatic route (Section 8), but the **underlying retail-sector FDI cap/route still applies** if the franchisor takes equity in the Indian operating entity. [Altacit](https://www.altacit.com/franchise-law-in-india-legal-framework-for-franchisors-franchisees-2026/)

**Practical implication:** because there's no franchise-specific statute or disclosure mandate, due diligence and contract quality (territory rights, termination, IP licensing, non-compete, dispute resolution venue) carry more legal weight in India than in more heavily-regulated franchise markets — and the same contract needs to independently satisfy Contract Act, Consumer Protection Act, and FEMA/FDI requirements simultaneously.

## 14. Distribution Models Common in India

### General Trade (GT) vs. Modern Trade (MT)
- **General Trade** (the traditional, unorganized "kirana"/mom-and-pop retail network) is reported to still account for **70–75% of FMCG sales** in India, and remains dominant in rural/semi-urban markets. **Modern Trade** covers organized formats — supermarkets, hypermarkets, specialty chains. [Massist CRM — General Trade vs Modern Trade](https://blog.massistcrm.com/general-trade-vs-modern-trade-vs-quick-commerce-in-fmcg-key-differences-growth-opportunities); [Kirana Club — FMCG Distribution India Guide 2026](https://kirana.club/resources/fmcg-distribution-india-guide)

### Typical GT distribution chain
**Manufacturer/Brand → C&F Agent (Carrying & Forwarding) → Superstockist → Redistribution Stockist/Distributor → Sub-stockist → Retailer**
- A **C&F Agent** stores and dispatches goods on the brand's behalf without taking ownership of the stock (the brand retains ownership); the C&F agent earns a **service fee, typically a percentage of the value of goods handled** (not a fixed remuneration), and often also handles regional tax/compliance logistics. A **distributor**, by contrast, actually **buys** the goods and resells to retailers — i.e., C&F is the logistics arm, distributor is the sales/credit arm. A **superstockist** typically covers a city/region and supplies multiple downstream distributors. [Arpit Srivastava — FMCG Distribution in India](https://arpitsrivastava.com/fmcg-distribution-in-india-hul-nestle-itc/); [SalesPort/Sortstring — C&F Agent meaning](https://sortstring.com/resources/what-is-cfa); [Godamwale — C&F Agents for FMCG](https://godamwale.com/c-and-f-agents-for-fmcg/)

### Modern Trade
- MT distribution is often more direct — supply from a distribution center straight to the retail chain's own warehouse/store, with automated/centralized replenishment systems rather than the multi-tier GT chain. [Massist CRM](https://blog.massistcrm.com/general-trade-vs-modern-trade-vs-quick-commerce-in-fmcg-key-differences-growth-opportunities)

### Quick Commerce (emerging modern channel)
- **Quick commerce** (Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart, plus Amazon Now, Flipkart Minutes, BigBasket's BB Now) has become a major and fast-growing channel, valued at roughly **₹640 billion / ~USD 7.28 billion in FY2025**, with projections to potentially triple by 2028, and reported monthly GMV around **₹11,000 crore in January 2026** — representing a distinct, dark-store-based distribution model brands increasingly must plan for alongside GT/MT. [GlobeNewswire — India Quick Commerce Report 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/20/3277255/28124/en/india-quick-commerce-report-2026-market-to-reach-12-97-billion-by-2029-blinkit-zepto-and-swiggy-instamart-lead-surge-as-jiomart-and-bigbasket-scale-competitive-entry.html); [StartupFeed — Quick Commerce War 2026](https://startupfeed.in/quick-commerce-war-2026-blinkit-zepto-instamart-amazon-flipkart/)

## 15. Consumer Behaviour and Regional/Language Diversity

- India is frequently characterized in market-entry literature as **"many markets within one"** — a single nationwide go-to-market approach tends to underperform because consumer behavior differs meaningfully by geography, language, income level, and culture (e.g., Mumbai vs. Madurai vs. Mandi consumer behavior differs substantially, per the cited source). [India Acclime — Regional Market Segmentation](https://india.acclime.com/guides/regional-market-segmentation/)
- India has **22 constitutionally scheduled languages and 121+ spoken languages** across **2,000+ distinct ethnic/cultural groups**, per secondary sourcing. An analysis of the 2011 Census is cited as finding roughly a **36% chance that two randomly selected Indians share a language they both understand** — illustrating the scale of the localization challenge. [AsiaLocalize — Languages Spoken in India](https://asialocalize.com/blog/languages-spoken-in-india/); [Behavioral Targeting — Language marketing strategies in India](https://behavioraltargeting.biz/what-language-should-be-used-when-marketing-in-india-localization-strategies/)
- Hindi is widely spoken/understood in northern states; southern states are predominantly **Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam** speaking — meaning national campaigns built only in Hindi/English risk under-resonating in the south. [AsiaLocalize](https://asialocalize.com/blog/languages-spoken-in-india/)
- **Localization is described as a prerequisite, not an optional add-on**, for brand relevance in India — extending beyond translation to product formulation/taste, pricing strategy, and culturally-aligned marketing messaging/creators. Brands that localize tone, packaging, and regional influencers reportedly outperform single, national-campaign approaches. [Lyxel&Flamingo — Power of Local Language Marketing in India](https://lyxelandflamingo.com/blogs/media-operations/the-power-of-local-language-marketing-in-india/)
- Most of India's **new internet users** are coming from Tier 2/Tier 3/rural geographies and show a strong preference for **native-language content** over English/Hindi, with higher trust and recall for regionally-tailored campaigns. [Ace Logic Solutions — Digital marketing for India market entry](https://www.acelogicsolutions.com/a-comprehensive-guide-to-digital-marketing-services-for-india-market-entry/)

India's federal structure means **many compliance obligations are set or administered at the state level**, even where a central law/policy exists. Concrete examples:

### Haryana
- Notified a new **industrial policy** (reported effective from 26 May 2026) featuring an **"AI Single Window 2.0" / Intelligent Investment Facilitation Portal** — an AI-enabled platform intended to bundle approvals, land allocation, incentives, and clearances into a single interface, explicitly positioned to compete with Karnataka and Maharashtra for investment (including data-centre/GCC capital). ### Maharashtra
- Retail/commercial premises must register under the **Maharashtra Shops and Establishments (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act, 2017** — commonly called the **"Gumasta License"** — within 30 days of commencing business; this covers offices, retail shops, warehouses, and e-commerce businesses, and governs working hours (48 hrs/week cap), weekly holidays, overtime pay, leave, maternity benefits, and child-labour prohibition. [IndiaFilings — Maharashtra Shops and Establishment Act](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/maharashtra-shops-and-establishment-act); [Deskera — Maharashtra Shops & Establishments Registration](https://www.deskera.com/blog/maharashtra-registration-shops-and-establishments/)

### Karnataka
- Retail/commercial premises must register under the **Karnataka Shops and Commercial Establishments Act, 1961**, within 30 days of commencing operations — applicable to essentially all commercial establishments regardless of size or employee count. [IndiaFilings — Karnataka Shops and Establishment Act](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/karnataka-shops-and-establishment-act); [Greythr — Karnataka Shops and Commercial Establishments Act](https://www.greythr.com/wiki/acts/karnataka-shops-commercial-establishments-act/)
- Karnataka levies **Professional Tax** on employers/self-employed/GST-registered businesses with more than five employees under the same Act — reported at **₹2,500/year** for self-employed/GST-registered entities, due by a set annual date (30 April cited for 2026). [GovtSchemes.org — Professional Tax in Karnataka 2026](https://govtschemes.org/professional-tax-in-karnataka-2026/)
- Karnataka's **Industrial Policy 2025–2030** revamped its **Single Window System**, allowing investors to apply for **150+ state-level services across 30+ departments** through one portal, with auto-pre-filled information. [Grant Thornton — Karnataka Industrial Policy 2025-30](https://www.grantthornton.in/link/a7d637f7d0484abeb7c2bea462ef96fb.aspx); [Deccan Herald — Karnataka all-in-one platform](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/karnataka-promises-all-in-one-platform-for-industries-876325.html)

**Takeaway:** Beyond the three examples above, **every Indian state has its own Shops & Establishments Act, professional tax regime (where applicable), and industrial/investment policy with different incentive menus** — meaning a pan-India retail rollout requires state-by-state compliance mapping, not a single national registration.

## 18. Digital Commerce Regulations

### E-commerce FDI policy
- **DPIIT Press Note No. 2 (2018 Series)**, effective **1 February 2019**, distinguishes between:
  - **Marketplace model** — an e-commerce entity provides an IT platform/facilitator between buyers and sellers (does not own inventory) — **100% FDI permitted under automatic route**, subject to conditions; and
  - **Inventory-based model** — the e-commerce entity owns the inventory and sells directly to consumers — **FDI is prohibited** in this model.
  [Mondaq — FDI in E-Commerce Press Note 2 2018](https://www.mondaq.com/india/fiscal-monetary-policy/768654/fdi-in-e-commerce-activities-press-note-no-2-2018-series); [Khaitan & Co — FDI in E-Commerce Press Note 2](https://www.khaitanco.com/thought-leadership/FDI-in-E-commerce-activities-press-note-no-2)
- Press Note 2 (2018) was specifically introduced to address concerns that marketplace e-commerce entities were **indirectly circumventing** the inventory-based FDI prohibition (e.g., through preferential/exclusive seller relationships or vendor equity control). [CBCL NLIU — FDI E-commerce guidelines reflection](https://cbcl.nliu.ac.in/contemporary-issues/fdi-e-commerce-guidelines-a-reflection-of-loopholes-and-repercussions/)
- **Requires further validation:** whether this framework has been materially amended since 2018/2019 — no confirmed post-2019 amendment to the core marketplace/inventory distinction was found in this research pass; verify directly against DPIIT's current Consolidated FDI Policy.

### Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020
- Notified under the **Consumer Protection Act, 2019**, effective **23 July 2020**; apply to **all e-commerce transactions involving Indian consumers, regardless of whether the e-commerce entity is based in India or abroad** — i.e., extraterritorial reach relevant to any foreign brand selling to Indian consumers online even without a local entity. [TeamLease RegTech — Consumer Protection E-Commerce Rules 2020](https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/blogs/134/e-commerce-compliance-in-india-understanding-the-consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020/); [Trilegal — Consumer Protection E-Commerce Rules 2020](https://trilegal.com/knowledge_repository/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020/)
- Key obligations: appoint an **India-resident Nodal Officer/grievance officer**; **acknowledge consumer complaints within 48 hours** and resolve within **1 month**; protect personally identifiable consumer information and not disclose it without consent. [Lexology — Consumer Protection E-Commerce Rules overview](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=40bbcb14-5b11-4625-9a9c-3367688367ab)
- Enforcement sits with the **Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA)**; non-compliance with CCPA directives can lead to criminal prosecution under **Section 88, Consumer Protection Act, 2019**. [ACM Legal — Consumer Protection E-Commerce Rules 2020](https://www.acmlegal.org/blog/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020/)

### Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act)
- Passed/enacted **August 2023**; applies to digital personal data processing **within India**, and **extraterritorially** to processing outside India where it involves offering goods/services to individuals in India — directly relevant to any international brand's e-commerce/CRM stack touching Indian consumer data. [India Briefing — DPDP Act 2023 key provisions](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-key-provisions-29021.html/)
- Core requirements: **explicit consent** for personal data processing (with limited carve-outs, e.g., national security/legal compliance); **age-verification mechanisms**, reportedly required broadly across companies (not just social platforms) for any child-data-adjacent processing. [Usercentrics — DPDP Act overview](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/india-digital-personal-data-protection-act-dpdpa/)
- **DPDP Rules** were reported to have rolled out in **phases starting around November 2025**, with **full compliance expected by 13 May 2027** — meaning this is an actively phasing-in regime as of this research date (July 2026). [Privacy World Blog — India DPDP Rules passed](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/11/india-passes-the-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-ushering-in-a-new-digital-age-in-india/)
- Reported penalties for e-commerce platforms: up to **₹250 crore** for security failures and **₹200 crore** for breach-notification lapses (*figures from a secondary compliance-advisory source; verify against the Act's Schedule of penalties directly*). [Ardent Privacy — DPDP Act impact on e-commerce](https://www.ardentprivacy.ai/blog/how-will-indias-dpdp-act-impact-e-commerce-businesses/)
- The DPDP Act operates **alongside** (not instead of) the Consumer Protection Act/E-Commerce Rules — creating a **dual compliance burden** for any digital consumer brand. [KSandK — DPDP Act and Consumer Protection interplay](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-act-consumer-protection-navigating-dual-compliance/)

**Practical implication:** A foreign brand selling into India purely via a website/app — with no Indian entity, no warehouse, no local retail — can still trigger compliance obligations under the Consumer Protection E-Commerce Rules and the DPDP Act purely by virtue of serving Indian consumers online. This is a materially different (and lower-friction, but not zero-friction) entry path compared with physical retail/FDI-regulated routes.
